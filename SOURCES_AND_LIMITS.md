# Scientific evidence and reuse

The early-leaf comparison follows Arabidopsis GL1, AT3G27920, transcript NM_113708.2. The modeled DNA is contiguous within exon three. It spans codons 91–96. Base five of the 18-base fragment is CDS position 275. Coding DNA TCT → TTT gives messenger RNA UCU → UUU and Ser92 → Phe92.

Dai et al. linked S92F to the early-leaf phenotype and impaired GL2 activation. GL3/EGL3 interactions were retained in the reported assays. The lesson models regulatory consequences. The returned atomic geometry belongs to DNA. Later leaves may bear marginal trichomes. [Dai et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.12695). [NCBI genetic code](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Taxonomy/Utils/wprintgc.cgi).

Each GL1 duplex is a sequence-specific regular B-DNA fiber model constructed with wDSSR model 4. Each contains 738 heavy atoms and 826 covalent bonds. A neutral label marks it as a structural model. The comparison reuses one reference frame so the backbone and surrounding positions stay aligned. Dashed guides indicate contacts between heavy atoms. Hydrogens, solvent, sequence-dependent relaxation and measured mutant conformations are outside the model. [wDSSR modeling documentation](https://web.x3dna-dssr.org/tutorial).

Suo, Seifert & Kirik, 2013, Figure 2A, CC BY 3.0, shows a separate wild-type specimen. Talbot & White, 2013, Figure 1B, CC BY 2.0, shows the enlarged surface and trichome base. These are crops of published panels retaining labels and scale bars. The imported, credited crops are in `runtime/TwoOaks/Assets/MicroscopyR7/`. Other retained panels carry their original credits. [Suo et al., Figure 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3830481/figure/F2/). [CC BY 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/). [Talbot & White, Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3853341/figure/F1/). [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/).

The tissue relief and puzzle-shaped wall contours follow measured propidium-iodide fluorescence in a separate Arabidopsis cotyledon. The opened cell contains an illustrative vacuole and peripheral nucleus. [Erguvan & Verger](https://zenodo.org/records/2577053). Its enlarged companion displays 68 measured optical sections through an independent Arabidopsis nucleus. The DNA stain shows chromatin distribution. It does not identify the position of GL1. [Schubert et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22476443/).

The rescue is labeled “Model experiment.” It illustrates complementation with a working gene copy and the development of a new leaf. The original variant remains as the control. [Herman & Marks, 1989](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC159842/).

Each whole leaf is linked to a separate enlarged surface detail. Both groups use the same closed epidermal relief. In the working-copy group, an illustrative trichome grows from a fixed footprint on that relief. Its basal rim follows the local slope around the entire cell. This separates the modeled developmental sequence from the published SEM images of separate specimens.

The laboratory reuses GENESIS room geometry and existing botanical reconstructions, lighting and visualization techniques. ## Spatial biology exhibits

The internal compartments and placement of pores on the nuclear envelope are anatomical illustrations. Pore shape comes from an independent root-protoplast map. [EMDB 54657](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/emdb/EMD-54657). The enlarged nucleus is reconstructed from one independent DAPI stack in the [Gaudin & Andrey dataset](https://doi.org/10.15454/1HSOIE). The 80S ribosome uses [PDB 9H3G](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/9H3G). The space-filling model uses the predicted heavy atoms of the GL1 reference domain from [AlphaFold DB P27900](https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk/entry/P27900), and the amino acids use wwPDB CCD geometry. The detailed sample identity, transformations, credits and scope are in `science/arabidopsis-imaging-r35/SOURCES.md`.

The outcome peel uses measured wall markings and reduced optical relief with a shallow illustrative dome. The mRNA path and growth sequence are educational representations.
